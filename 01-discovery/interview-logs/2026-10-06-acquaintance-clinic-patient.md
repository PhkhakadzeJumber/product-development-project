# Interview log: acquaintance of Luka's, long-term patient at one clinic

**We are wrong if:** people tell us they got their answer on the first call or message, or cannot remember a time they needed their doctor between visits.

**Date:** 06.10.2026 · **Asker:** Luka Mikautadze · **Logger:** Luka Mikautadze (solo call, no second teammate) · **Where / how:** phone
**Who they are:** an acquaintance, goes to the same doctor at the same clinic for four or five years now. Not in this course. Interview was in Georgian, translated by me.

## What happened (their story, in order)
- A few months ago he got test results and a new medication. At home he was not sure when to take one of the medicines.
- He called the clinic: no answer. Called again later: no answer. Third call, reception said the doctor was with a patient and would pass on the message.
- Nobody called back that day. Next day he called again and explained again; the doctor called him back later that day and explained.
- Hardest time: waiting for test results with questions and the doctor unavailable. "The difficult part was just not knowing."
- Going in person is the fallback: last visit he waited 20 to 30 minutes for a 15 to 20 minute appointment.
- Booking an appointment itself is easy: one call, a choice of slots within a few days.

## Verbatim quotes (at least 3, word for word)
1. "I first called the clinic. They didn't answer, so I called again later. The third time somebody answered and told me the doctor was with another patient and that they would pass on the message."
2. "The difficult part was just not knowing. You don't want to keep calling them, but at the same time you don't want to wait several days without knowing what's going on."
3. "I think I called maybe five or six times over two days. Probably an hour or so altogether, including waiting and talking to reception."
4. "I've also asked the doctor before whether I can call directly, but normally they don't give you a direct number."
5. "Sometimes I just wait until the next appointment if it's not urgent."

## Numbers they gave
- Calls: 5 or 6 over 2 days · Trips: 0 (would have gone back if calls failed, "a couple of hours with travelling and waiting") · Hours: about 1 on the phone · Money: none

## What they do about it today
- Calls reception, sometimes asks them to leave a message for the doctor.
- Has asked for a direct number; was not given one.
- If not urgent, waits until the next appointment.

## Did this support or weaken the hypothesis?
Supports it. He needed the doctor between visits, did not get an answer on the first call, and it took 5 or 6 calls over two days (quotes 1 and 3). The unpicked beliefs look weaker for him: waiting at the clinic was 20 to 30 minutes, and getting an appointment took one call.

# Interview log: acquaintance of Luka's, doctor at a public polyclinic

**We are wrong if:** patients generally get appointments easily, reach the hospital without significant difficulty, see their doctor without long waits, and do not feel that the process takes too much of their time and financial effort. We are also wrong if doctors generally do not experience large numbers of patients as stressful or disruptive to their work.

**Date:** 06.10.2026 · **Asker:** Luka · **Logger(s):** Jumber, Giorgi · **Where / how:** phone · **Length:** about 25 minutes, evening after clinic closed
**Who they are:** an acquaintance of Luka's, a general practitioner at a public polyclinic in Kutaisi for about eight years. Not in this course. Spoke Georgian, translated by Luka.

## Core (for doctors)

**10. How do you generally feel about your job at the hospital?**
He likes the medical part and the patients he has known for years. What wears him down is the pace: by the afternoon he is rushing people he would like to give more time to.

**11. Walk me through the last day when you had a particularly large number of patients.**
A Monday two weeks ago. He had 22 booked patients and 9 more came without an appointment, 31 in total. He started at 9, was supposed to finish at 3, and left after 5 without a lunch break.

**12. Tell me about the last time the number of patients affected how you worked.**
That Monday. He cut booked appointments from about 15 minutes to under 10, and wrote the notes for the last patients the next morning before the clinic opened.

**13. How many patients do you usually have daily on average?**
About 20 to 25 booked, and on most days 5 to 8 more without an appointment.

**14. What usually happens when more patients arrive than you expect?**
Reception sends them to sit outside his door and he takes them in the gaps between booked patients. The booked patients then go in late and the corridor fills up. Anyone who looks unwell goes first.

**15. What part of your work becomes most difficult when there are many patients?**
Explaining and paperwork. Every visit has to be entered into the electronic system, and on busy days he does that after hours. He also has no time to explain a new medicine properly.

**16. Tell me about the last time many people arrived around the same time?**
That same Monday morning. People from the villages come on the first bus and all arrive around 9. By 9:30 there were about ten people outside his door before the first booked patient had left.

## Close

**17. Thinking about everything we've talked about, is there anything important about dealing with doctors or hospitals that I didn't ask about?**
Only that many walk-ins have a two-minute question and could not reach him by phone. Brief overlap, not his main story.

**18. Who else should we talk to about this?**
The receptionist at his polyclinic, who sees everyone who comes without an appointment. Luka to ask for an introduction.

**19. Could we come back when we have something to show you?**
Yes, by phone, in the evening after the clinic closes.

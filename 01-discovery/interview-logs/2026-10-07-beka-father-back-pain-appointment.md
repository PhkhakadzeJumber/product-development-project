# Interview log: Beka's father, garage owner with back pain needing an appointment

**We are wrong if:** patients generally get appointments easily, reach the hospital without significant difficulty, see their doctor without long waits, and do not feel that the process takes too much of their time and financial effort. We are also wrong if doctors generally do not experience large numbers of patients as stressful or disruptive to their work.

**Date:** 07.10.2026 · **Asker:** Beka · **Logger(s):** Giorgi, Luka · **Where / how:** in person, at the garage office · **Length:** about 30 minutes
**Who they are:** Beka's father, owns a small car repair garage in Kutaisi, needed to see a doctor at a public clinic in Kutaisi for back pain. Not in this course. Spoke Georgian, translated by Beka.

## Warm-up

**1. How often do you usually need to visit a doctor or hospital?**
Rarely. This was the first time in about a year he needed his regular doctor for himself.

## Core

**2. Walk me through the last time you had to go to the doctor, from when you decided to go until you left the hospital.**
His back hurt for a week and he could not lift at the garage. He called the clinic registry for three days to get an appointment, could not get through, then went there in person. The registry told him the next free slot with his doctor was in two weeks. He waited two weeks in pain, then went, waited about 30 minutes that day, was seen for about 10 minutes and got exercises and a referral.

**3. How long did it take you to get to the hospital that day?**
About 20 minutes each way by car. Minor overlap only.

**4. How long did it take you to wait in the waiting hall until you saw the doctor?**
About 30 minutes on the final visit day. Not the pain — booking was.

**5. Tell me about the last time you had to wait a long time to see a doctor.**
Not this story. The long part was the two weeks waiting for the slot, not the corridor that day.

**6. Tell me about the last time you had difficulty getting an appointment with a doctor.**
This one. He called the registry for three days — morning and afternoon — line busy or no answer. On day three he closed the garage for an hour and went in person. Registry book showed the doctor full: next free in two weeks. He took it.

**7. What did you have to do while waiting to see the doctor?**
For two weeks: worked half-strength, asked a neighbour to help lift. On the visit day: sat in the corridor 30 minutes.

**8. What did the last hospital visit cost you in terms of your time or other things you had to give up?**
Three days of calling, one wasted in-person trip to book, two weeks working in pain, plus a lost working morning at the garage when he finally went.

**9. Tell me about the busiest day you have experienced at a hospital.**
Not discussed. His story is about getting in, not the corridor.

## Close

**17. Thinking about everything we've talked about, is there anything important about dealing with doctors or hospitals that I didn't ask about?**
That you cannot plan: "If the book is full, you wait in pain. What else can you do?"

**18. Who else should we talk to about this?**
A customer at the garage who also waited weeks for a clinic slot. Beka to follow up.

**19. Could we come back when we have something to show you?**
Yes, at the garage.

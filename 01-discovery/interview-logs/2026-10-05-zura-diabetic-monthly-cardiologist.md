# Interview log: Zura, diabetic seen once a month by one cardiologist at a polyclinic

**We are wrong if:** patients generally get appointments easily, reach the hospital without significant difficulty, see their doctor without long waits, and do not feel that the process takes too much of their time and financial effort. We are also wrong if doctors generally do not experience large numbers of patients as stressful or disruptive to their work.

**Date:** 05.10.2026 · **Asker:** Giorgi · **Logger(s):** Beka, Jumber · **Where / how:** in person, his flat · **Length:** about 35 minutes, with tea
**Who they are:** diabetic for four or five years, same cardiologist at a public polyclinic once a month. Keeps a sugar diary, she checks it and writes the prescription. Runs a shop. Giorgi's neighbour. Not in this course. Spoke Georgian, translated by Giorgi.

## Warm-up

**1. How often do you usually need to visit a doctor or hospital?**
Once a month, the same cardiologist at a public polyclinic.

## Core

**2. Walk me through the last time you had to go to the doctor, from when you decided to go until you left the hospital.**
In August the cardiologist swapped his tablet for a new one. He took it in the morning and by eleven he was shaking and sweating, and measured 3.4. He called the clinic, then went without an appointment on day three after reception told him to come in. Forty minutes by bus each way, about two hours in the corridor, three minutes with the doctor for a half-tablet correction. Left home at nine, back by one.

**3. How long did it take you to get to the hospital that day?**
Forty minutes by bus each way, 2 GEL each way.

**4. How long did it take you to wait in the waiting hall until you saw the doctor?**
About two hours in the corridor outside her room, waiting for a gap between booked patients.

**5. Tell me about the last time you had to wait a long time to see a doctor.**
That visit: about two hours for three minutes with the doctor. With an appointment he waits twenty to thirty minutes and calls that normal.

**6. Tell me about the last time you had difficulty getting an appointment with a doctor.**
Booking is fine — the doctor books his next visit before he leaves. This time the issue was reaching her between visits before coming in.

**7. What did you have to do while waiting to see the doctor?**
Sat in the corridor outside her room.

**8. What did the last hospital visit cost you in terms of your time or other things you had to give up?**
About four hours door to door, 4 GEL for transport, and he did not open the shop that morning: "fifty, eighty lari I didn't make, who knows."

**9. Tell me about the busiest day you have experienced at a hospital.**
That same day. Corridor full, booked patients going in late.

## Close

**17. Thinking about everything we've talked about, is there anything important about dealing with doctors or hospitals that I didn't ask about?**
"Only that the girl at reception decides everything. If she likes you, she passes the message. If not, nothing."

**18. Who else should we talk to about this?**
Dato, same cardiologist, "we were in the same ward years ago, he'll talk, he likes to talk." Zura is passing on Giorgi's number. Giorgi follows up.

**19. Could we come back when we have something to show you?**
Yes. He said we can come back when we have something to show him.

- Adjusts the dose himself from the old tablets in the drawer.

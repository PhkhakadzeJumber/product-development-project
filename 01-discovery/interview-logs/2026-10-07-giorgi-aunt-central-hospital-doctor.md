# Interview log: Giorgi's aunt, doctor at Kutaisi Central Hospital

**We are wrong if:** patients generally get appointments easily, reach the hospital without significant difficulty, see their doctor without long waits, and do not feel that the process takes too much of their time and financial effort. We are also wrong if doctors generally do not experience large numbers of patients as stressful or disruptive to their work.

**Date:** 07.10.2026 · **Asker:** Giorgi · **Logger(s):** Beka, Jumber · **Where / how:** phone, evening · **Length:** about 30 minutes
**Who they are:** Giorgi's aunt, doctor at Kutaisi Central Hospital. Not in this course. Spoke Georgian, translated by Giorgi.

## Core (for doctors)

**10. How do you generally feel about your job at the hospital?**
She said she liked medicine but no longer enjoys the job. What breaks her is not one busy Monday but every day short-staffed: "great number of patients and low number of colleagues daily." She wants to quit.

**11. Walk me through the last day when you had a particularly large number of patients.**
Last Saturday night shift. Two doctors were supposed to cover the ward, one called in sick, so she covered alone with the nurses. Patients kept coming from the emergency door all night, she worked 12 hours with one short tea break and handed over cases still unfinished in the morning.

**12. Tell me about the last time the number of patients affected how you worked.**
That Saturday. She skipped full examinations to triage, postponed all discharge papers to the next day, and called the morning doctor back for a value she had no time to double-check.

**13. How many patients do you usually have daily on average?**
Ward-dependent, but on short days one doctor covers what two or three should. She said long hours are normal now, not exceptional.

**14. What usually happens when more patients arrive than you expect?**
There is nobody to send them to. The waiting area fills, nurses prioritise who looks worst, and the rest wait. Different from the polyclinic — no gaps to take them in, just a queue that outlasts the shift.

**15. What part of your work becomes most difficult when there are many patients?**
Staying calm and kind. Paperwork second. She said: "You work tirelessly all day and still leave people waiting. Then you go home and think about quitting."

**16. Tell me about the last time many people arrived around the same time?**
That Saturday around midnight. Three arrivals within half an hour after a quiet evening, all while she was alone with two nurses.

## Close

**17. Thinking about everything we've talked about, is there anything important about dealing with doctors or hospitals that I didn't ask about?**
That patients see the wait, doctors see the understaffing. Same corridor, other side.

**18. Who else should we talk to about this?**
A nurse on her ward who sees the same short-staffed shifts. Giorgi to ask for an introduction.

**19. Could we come back when we have something to show you?**
Yes, by phone in the evening.

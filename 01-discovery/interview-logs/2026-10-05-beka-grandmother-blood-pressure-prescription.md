# Interview log: Beka's grandmother, monthly blood pressure prescription at a clinic

**We are wrong if:** patients generally get appointments easily, reach the hospital without significant difficulty, see their doctor without long waits, and do not feel that the process takes too much of their time and financial effort. We are also wrong if doctors generally do not experience large numbers of patients as stressful or disruptive to their work.

**Date:** 05.10.2026 · **Asker:** Beka · **Logger(s):** Giorgi · **Where / how:** in person, her flat · **Length:** about 35 minutes, with tea

**Who they are:** 72, hypertension, same prescription for years, same family doctor at a district clinic. Beka's grandmother. Not in this course. Spoke Georgian, translated by Beka. Giorgi watched for places where Beka answered for her.

## Warm-up

**1. How often do you usually need to visit a doctor or hospital?**
Every month to renew her blood pressure prescription.

## Core

**2. Walk me through the last time you had to go to the doctor, from when you decided to go until you left the hospital.**
She needed the same monthly prescription. She went to the clinic by bus, waited about two hours with an appointment because another patient was still with the doctor, then had to leave for something else without seeing the doctor and without the prescription. Has to go again.

**3. How long did it take you to get to the hospital that day?**
About an hour each way by bus.

**4. How long did it take you to wait in the waiting hall until you saw the doctor?**
She waited about two hours but left without seeing the doctor.

**5. Tell me about the last time you had to wait a long time to see a doctor.**
This visit. She had an appointment but waited about two hours because another patient was still with the doctor. Sometimes several patients are already waiting when she arrives.

**6. Tell me about the last time you had difficulty getting an appointment with a doctor.**
She tried several times to find a slot that fit her schedule. Minor overlap only — booking was not the main pain that day.

**7. What did you have to do while waiting to see the doctor?**
Sat in the clinic corridor until she had to leave for something else.

**8. What did the last hospital visit cost you in terms of your time or other things you had to give up?**
About four hours travel plus two hours waiting — half a day — plus bus fare, with nothing to show for it. Because she left without the prescription, she must spend the same again: "every month half a day, and this month twice."

**9. Tell me about the busiest day you have experienced at a hospital.**
Not a separate day — this one. She said the corridor was already full when she arrived.

## Close

**17. Thinking about everything we've talked about, is there anything important about dealing with doctors or hospitals that I didn't ask about?**
She said that having an appointment does not necessarily mean she knows when she will actually see the doctor.

**18. Who else should we talk to about this?**
A neighbour who also visits the same clinic and has experienced long waits. Beka to follow up at Sunday lunch.

**19. Could we come back when we have something to show you?**
Yes, at her flat.

## What they do about it today
- Goes in person every month even though the prescription never changes.
- If the doctor is out or the wait is too long, leaves and comes again.
- Asks Beka to call ahead when she can.

## Did this support or weaken the hypothesis?
Supports the financial-repeat hero: same no-exam prescription trip every month costs half a day plus fare, doubled when she leaves unseen. Travel and wait are the mechanism, cost is the pain.

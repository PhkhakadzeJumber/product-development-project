# Competitive Landscape

**Source:** the nine completed interview logs in `01-discovery/interview-logs/`. This landscape records only workarounds and alternatives named in those logs; quotes remain in the interview logs' translated wording.

## What interviewees actually use today

| Workaround or alternative (as they named it) | The job it does for them | Logs that mention it | What it fails at, in their words (quote + log number) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Regular in-person visits to the same clinic/doctor | Get recurring prescriptions, check-ups, test review, and treatment adjustments. | 01, 02, 03, 05, 07 | “I had an appointment, but what does that mean? I still waited.” (01); “I sat an hour and a half for five minutes.” (02) |
| Calling the clinic/ward or asking reception to pass a message | Book or confirm visits and reach a doctor between visits. | 02, 03, 04, 05, 07, 09 | “Nothing happened. It rang. Nobody picked up.” (03); “I called three days, nobody picks up.” (05); “The difficult part was just not knowing.” (07); from the desk: “I can see the numbers piling up on the screen, but I have a line of sick people staring at me.” (09) |
| Reception transferring the call to the doctor's room, or leaving a handwritten note on the doctor's desk (the head nurse sometimes calls back from the notes at the end of the shift) | Get a patient's question to the doctor when the doctor is with a patient. | 03, 09 | “I transfer the call, but ninety percent of the time the doctor doesn't pick up because they have someone on the couch.” (09); “If she likes you, she passes the message. If not, nothing.” (03); notes are rarely called back the same day (09; described, not a verbatim quote). |
| Going to the clinic registry in person to book | Get an appointment when calls to the registry do not get through. | 05 | “I closed the garage for an hour just to book.” (05); “If the book is full, you wait in pain.” (05) |
| A relative's help (calling ahead, driving, or looking for information) | Help a patient communicate with the clinic, get to care, or find information. | 01, 02, 03 | “My daughter had to miss work to bring me.” (02). The online search by the patient's daughter found “nothing useful.” (03; outcome recorded in the log, not as a verbatim quote.) |
| Using an old tablet and changing the dose without the doctor's advice | Manage a medication problem while waiting to reach the doctor. | 03 | “I know you shouldn't do that. But what do I do, sit and shake?” (03) |
| Waiting until the next appointment for a non-urgent question | Avoid another trip or interruption when a question can wait. | 03, 07 | “The tablet, you can't wait a month with 3.4.” (03); “Sometimes I just wait until the next appointment if it's not urgent.” (07) |
| Taking walk-ins between booked patients | Let patients without appointments be seen without displacing urgent cases entirely. | 04, 09 | “By the afternoon I am rushing people I want to give more time to.” (04); booked patients also “go in late” (04; described by the doctor, not a verbatim quote); one walk-in slipped in at 2:50 PM made the next two booked patients each start 15 minutes late (09; described, not a verbatim quote). |
| Triage and postponing examinations or paperwork when short-staffed | Prioritize the sickest patients and keep care moving through an understaffed hospital shift. | 06 | “You work tirelessly all day and still leave people waiting.” (06) |

## The strongest incumbent

Regular scheduled visits with a familiar doctor hold the routine-care job most firmly: they recur in logs 01, 02, 03, 05, and 07, and patients keep returning for prescriptions, monitoring, and treatment. For patients who can book ahead, the routine path can work—02's regular visits are booked ahead and 03 books the next visit before leaving—so the clearest failures are long waits or difficulty reaching the doctor between visits (01, 02, 03, 07).

## Alternatives nobody mentioned

No competitor app, telehealth service, or product brand was named in these logs. Log 03 says a patient's daughter searched online and found nothing useful, but does not identify a specific service or product.

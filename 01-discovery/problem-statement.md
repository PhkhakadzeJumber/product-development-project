# Problem Statement

## The statement

1. **Who:** People with an ongoing condition who see the same doctor at a public clinic or hospital on a regular rhythm, such as a diabetic seen once a month by one cardiologist, a heart-failure patient checked on the ward every two weeks, and a patient who has gone to the same doctor for four or five years. (02, 03, 07)
2. **When:** In the days after the doctor changes a medicine or a new result comes in, when something feels wrong at home (shaking with a sugar of 3.4 at eleven in the morning, 2 kg gained with swollen ankles, not knowing when to take a new tablet) and they phone the clinic to ask their doctor. (02, 03, 07)
3. **Cost:** 2, 6 and 4 calls over up to three days before any answer; 2 of the 3 then came in without an appointment and waited 1.5–2 hours in the corridor for a 3–5 minute answer, about four hours door to door with 4 GEL in transport and a lost shop morning (03) or a daughter's half day off work (02), while the third spent about an hour on the phone over two days. (02, 03, 07)
4. **Today:** They call reception again and again and ask it to pass a message, ask for the doctor's direct number and are refused, adjust the dose themselves while they wait, and when nobody calls back they come in without an appointment and are seen in the gaps between booked patients; a question that is not urgent waits for the next visit. (02, 03, 04, 07, 09)
5. **Why it fails:** Every route to the doctor goes through the reception line, and when it rings out there is no callback and no other way in: “Nothing happened. It rang. Nobody picked up.” and “I know you shouldn't do that. But what do I do, sit and shake?” (03); from the desk, 18 missed calls by 10:30 on a Monday and “I can see the numbers piling up on the screen, but I have a line of sick people staring at me.” (09)

## What the evidence ruled out

We believed the visit itself was the problem: two to four hours in the waiting area. Week 3 narrowed that to about 1.5–2 hours for 3 of 4 patients, with one at 30 minutes, and the two long waits in 02 and 03 came after unanswered calls, on visits without an appointment. With an appointment Zura waits 20 to 30 minutes and calls it normal, Gocha says his booked visits are fine, and booking was easy in 02, 03 and 07; only 05 struggled to get an appointment. The cost of a visit is real but mostly follows from not reaching the doctor, so it stays the runner-up. (01, 02, 03, 05, 07)

## What is still open

How many of the messages that do reach the doctor, as handwritten notes on the desk, ever get a call back, and how long after. Log 09 showed why the calls go unanswered (missed calls with no callback, transfers the doctor cannot take) but not what happens to the notes; the receptionist said the head nurse sometimes tries to call back at the end of the shift. It is the first question for the head nurse. (09)

## Pattern behind it

- **Pattern:** When something changes between visits, patients cannot reach the doctor by phone, so they keep calling for days or come in person. (02, 03, 04, 05, 07, 09)
- **Root cause:** All between-visit contact funnels through one reception line that cannot answer or return the calls, and the messages that do get through wait on paper on a busy doctor's desk, so any change forces a physical trip. (02, 03, 04, 07, 09)

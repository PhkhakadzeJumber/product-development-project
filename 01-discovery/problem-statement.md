# Problem Statement

## Who has the problem
Patients with a long-running illness and one regular doctor at a public clinic or hospital, especially those who live far from it. These are not "patients" in general — they are specific people we interviewed, each with a standing treatment: Beka's grandmother (72, monthly blood pressure prescription, district clinic) (01); Gocha (~60, heart failure, ward check-up every two weeks) (02); Zura (diabetic, monthly cardiologist at a polyclinic) (03); Beka's father (garage owner, back pain, public clinic) (05); and Luka's acquaintance, a long-term patient of one doctor at a clinic (07).

## When it happens
On the day of the visit, when they arrive to a corridor already full of patients and the appointment slip does not guarantee being seen on time — booked patients go in late while walk-ins are taken in the gaps (01, 02). Between visits, when something changes with their condition and they cannot reach the doctor by phone — the line rings with no pickup, nobody calls back, and the only fallback offered is coming in person (03, 05, 07).

## What it costs them
A 3- to 10-minute visit costs half a day: travel each way plus a corridor wait (01, 02, 03, 05). Beka's grandmother spends about an hour each way by bus, waits two hours, and left without her prescription — "every month half a day, and this month twice" (01). Zura spends forty minutes by bus each way, about two hours waiting, three minutes with the doctor, and loses his shop morning — "fifty, eighty lari I didn't make, who knows" (03). Gocha's daughter takes a half day off work to drive him (02). Beka's father works half-strength for two weeks in pain, closes his garage for an hour just to book, and loses a working morning (05). Between-visit questions that are not urgent wait for the next visit, and the patient lives with not knowing until then (03, 07).

## Evidence
Log numbers: (01) Beka's grandmother, (02) Gocha, (03) Zura, (04) Luka's acquaintance the polyclinic doctor, (05) Beka's father, (06) Giorgi's aunt, (07) Luka's acquaintance the long-term clinic patient.

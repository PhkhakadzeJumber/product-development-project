# Interview Script v1

**Problem we are investigating:** People who are ill cannot get an answer from their doctor between visits without calling again and again or going back to the clinic in person.
**Our prediction:** We are wrong if people tell us they got their answer on the first call or message, or cannot remember a time they needed their doctor between visits. (Never say this aloud.)

## Opening (2 min, word for word)
"I'm [name], a student at KIU, and this is my teammate [name]. We're doing a university project about how people deal with their doctors and clinics. We're not selling anything and we have nothing to show you. We just want to hear what actually happened to you, so there are no wrong answers. Is it okay if my teammate takes notes? We won't use your name."

## Warm-up (2 min)
1. When was the last time you went to a doctor, and which doctor did you see?
   - How long have you been going to that doctor?
   - How often do you normally see them?

## Core (12 to 15 min): your own words, your own problem

2. Think about the last time you had a question for your doctor after you'd already left the clinic. What happened?
   - What was the question?
   - What did you do first? What happened after that?
   - Who did you contact?
   - How did you eventually get the answer? How long did it take?
   - Keep asking about this one incident, not how things usually work.

3. Can you remember the most recent time you really needed an answer from your doctor but couldn't get one quickly?
   - What happened? Why did you need the answer?
   - What did you do when you couldn't reach them? What happened next?
   - How long did you end up waiting?

4. For that situation, how many times did you try to contact the clinic or doctor?
   - How many calls did you make?
   - Did you have to go to the clinic? How many trips?
   - About how much time did you spend dealing with it?
   - Did it cost you any money?
   - Push for numbers. "A few times" → "About how many? Three? Five? More?" "It took a while" → "Roughly how many hours?"

5. The last time this happened, what did you do to try to get an answer faster?
   - Have you tried anything else before? What do you normally do?
   - Why do you use that method? When was the last time you used it?
   - Did it work? What happened when it didn't?
   - Can you show me? (call log, messages, notes)

6. Think about the last time you went to the clinic. Walk me through what happened from when you arrived until you saw the doctor. *(belief we did not pick: the real pain is the waiting)*
   - What happened when you arrived? Did you have to check in?
   - How long did you wait? What happened while you were waiting?
   - What happened when you finally saw the doctor? How long was the appointment?

7. Think about the last time you needed an appointment. How did you book it? *(belief we did not pick: the real pain is getting an appointment at all)*
   - When did you book it? How many days did you wait for the appointment?
   - Did you have to contact anyone first? Did you try another way before booking it?
   - What made you use that method?

8. Only if there is still time: can you tell me about another recent time you had trouble getting information from your doctor or clinic?
   - When was that? What happened? What did you do?
   - How long did it take? What happened in the end?
   - This checks that our findings aren't based on one unusual incident.

## Close (2 min)
- Thinking about everything we've talked about, is there anything important about dealing with doctors or clinics that I didn't ask about?
- Who else should we talk to about this?
- Could we come back when we have something to show you?

## Follow-up bank
- When was the last time? · What happened? · What did you do? · What happened next? · How many times? · How long?
- And then what happened? · Why was that a problem? · Can you show me? · Silence: count to three.
- They say something general ("It's always difficult"): ask "When was the last time it happened?"

## Rules during the interview
- Ask about real events and what they actually did, never about what they might do in the future.
- Never mention the app or any solution.
- Do not ask: "Would you use an app for this?", "Would it be useful if you could message your doctor?", "Do you think this is a big problem?", "Would you pay for it?", "Do you think other people have this problem?", "What should we build?"
- When they stop talking, wait three seconds before speaking.

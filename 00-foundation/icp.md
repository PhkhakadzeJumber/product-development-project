# Ideal Customer Profile

One person, not a segment. If a stranger could not pick them out of a room from this page, rewrite it.

**Name (invented) and one-line sketch:** Gela, 56, has a long-running illness and one regular doctor at a public clinic, and lives 20 bus stops away from it

**Their situation:** Gela sees the same doctor every few weeks. Each visit means getting an appointment, a long ride to the clinic and a wait outside the doctor's room. Because he must keep coming back, repeated visits add up: transport fare, missed work, and paying for each visit and tests. Between visits his only ways to reach the doctor are the clinic's phone line or going there in person.

**The moment the problem shows up:** When the day of the visit comes he not only has to travel long distance to get to the hospital, but often waits around 2 hours to see the doctor. So, even though he arrives on time, he wastes a lot of time still due to the great number of patients and being far away from the hospital. Sometimes the long part is getting the slot itself: days of calling and weeks waiting in pain before the visit day.

**What it costs them:** Half a day for a few minutes with the doctor, plus bus/taxi fare each time (doubled when they leave unseen and must return), a lost shop or garage morning, and sometimes a relative's working day too. The wasted time and effort affects his mental and physical health, he feels bored and frustrated.

**What they do about it today:** he goes anyway and waits, comes without an appointment when he cannot get one that fits (after calls that go unanswered), or leaves without being seen and comes again. He tries to align visits with free time so as not to miss work.

**Evidence lines inside the profile:**
- Gela waits about two hours for a gap between booked patients, and the appointment slip does not guarantee being seen — Gocha waited 1.5 hours for five minutes (02), Zura waited two hours for three minutes (03), Beka's grandmother waited two hours with an appointment and left unseen (01).
- Repeated visits add up to real money: transport, missed work, paying for each visit — Beka's grandmother loses half a day every month, doubled when she leaves unseen (01); Zura loses his shop morning and 4 GEL transport (03); Gocha's daughter takes a half day off work (02).
- Between-visit contact funnels through an overloaded reception line with no callback — Zura called six times over three days with no answer (03), Beka's father called six times over three days and closed his garage for an hour to book in person (05).

**What they are NOT:**
- Someone with an emergency. They call 112 or go to the emergency room; waiting and wasting time is not their issue in this case.
- Someone with a doctor in the family. They already get the treatment they need with the help of their family member.

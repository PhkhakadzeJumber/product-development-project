# Four Filters Scorecard

Scored 06.10.2026, after four real interviews: three patients and one doctor. Updated 07.10.2026 after six real interviews: four patients and two doctors. Shortlist of three from the [problem pool](problem-pool.md), all from the same world: patients with a regular doctor at a public clinic or hospital in Kutaisi, and the doctors who see them. The pick is to **investigate**, not to build. The interviews can still kill it.

Each filter is scored 1 to 5. Evidence is a link to a log or a pool statement, never an opinion.

## The filters
1. **Observed.** Did we see it happen, and does it keep happening to the same people?
2. **Reachable.** Can we sit with five of these people this week, without going through an institution?
3. **Costly.** What does one occurrence cost them in time, money or risk, and how often does it occur?
4. **Testable.** Can a team of four students learn whether a solution helps within this semester?

### A. The time a visit costs, and the doctor's patient load

- **Observed, 5.** Four patients, each with a specific recent visit. [Gocha](../01-discovery/interview-logs/2026-10-05-gocha-heart-patient-ward.md): about 1.5 hours in the ward corridor for about 5 minutes with the doctor. [Beka's grandmother](../01-discovery/interview-logs/2026-10-05-beka-grandmother-blood-pressure-prescription.md): about two hours of waiting with an appointment, and she left without seeing the doctor. [Zura](../01-discovery/interview-logs/2026-10-05-zura-diabetic-monthly-cardiologist.md): about two hours in the corridor for three minutes with the doctor. [Beka's father](../01-discovery/interview-logs/2026-10-07-beka-father-back-pain-appointment.md): the wait was only 30 minutes that day — his pain was 3 days of calling plus a wasted booking trip plus 2 weeks waiting for the slot, slip shown. The doctor's side: [Luka's acquaintance](../01-discovery/interview-logs/2026-10-06-acquaintance-polyclinic-doctor.md) had 22 booked patients and 9 more without an appointment on his last bad Monday, and says it was like that on three of the last four Mondays; [Giorgi's aunt](../01-discovery/interview-logs/2026-10-07-giorgi-aunt-central-hospital-doctor.md) covered a 12-hour night shift alone (2 doctors became 1 sick), with at least one short-staffed shift every week. Pool statements from Jumber, Giorgi (his aunt and his neighbour Nani), Luka (the same acquaintance) and Beka (his father and his grandmother) say the same.
- **Reachable, 4.** Every patient came through a neighbour, a relative or a friend, with no clinic involved, and each interview produced a referral (see the [outreach tracker](../01-discovery/outreach-tracker.md)). Six done: Gocha, Zura, Beka's grandmother, Beka's father, plus two doctors by phone (Luka's acquaintance, Giorgi's aunt).
- **Costly, 4.** Gocha: about 3 hours plus his daughter's half day off work. Beka's grandmother: about an hour each way by bus and two hours of waiting, with nothing to show for it — doubled this month. Zura: about 4 hours door to door, 4 GEL transport and a lost shop morning (50-80 GEL). Beka's father: 3 days of calling, 1 wasted booking trip (closed the garage for an hour), 2 weeks working in pain, plus a lost working morning. The polyclinic doctor: appointments cut from about 15 minutes to under 10, no lunch break, and about two hours past the end of his shift. Giorgi's aunt: 12 hours with one short break, unfinished handover, thinks about quitting.
- **Testable, 3.** Minutes in the corridor and hours door to door are easy to measure for one visit. But queueing is the clinic's process, so changing it needs a clinic on board, and so far we have one doctor and no clinic.

### B. Answers between visits

- **Observed, 3.** Two of three patients. Gocha called the ward twice, nobody called back, and he says the hard part is getting help between visits. Zura called six times over three days and showed us the call log. The doctor says many of the people who come without an appointment have a two or three minute question and could not reach him by phone. Luka's pool statement about his uncle is the same story. Beka's grandmother did not mention it.
- **Reachable, 4.** The same people as A.
- **Costly, 3.** The cost shows up as the unplanned trip in A: Gocha and Zura both came in without an appointment because nobody answered. Zura also took half of an old tablet while he waited.
- **Testable, 4.** The incident has a clear start (a call that goes unanswered) and a clear end (an answer), so time-to-answer and number of calls can be measured.

### C. The monthly prescription trip

- **Observed, 3.** One person so far, Beka's grandmother: every month in person to renew the same prescription (pool statement and her log). Nobody else in the four interviews mentioned it.
- **Reachable, 2.** Older patients on a standing prescription, reached through family. We have one, and her neighbour at the same clinic as a possible second.
- **Costly, 3.** Half a day and transport once a month, doubled when she leaves without the prescription. Predictable, so people plan around it rather than suffer it.
- **Testable, 2.** Prescription renewal rules belong to the clinic and the pharmacy, and we do not yet know what a doctor is allowed to renew without seeing the patient.

## Pick and runner-up

**Pick: A, the time a visit costs.** Three of four patients with a wait of 1.5 to 2 hours and about half a day lost, one (Beka's father) with a 2-week wait for the slot and only 30 minutes in the corridor, and two doctors who describe the same corridor from inside the room: volume-flow (31 in a day, 2h overtime) and staffing-exhaustion (12h alone, wants to quit). Recorded in [DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md).

**Runner-up: B, answers between visits.** Same people and the same clinics, so it stays open at zero extra cost. It may turn out to be the cause of A: Gocha and Zura only waited that long because they came without an appointment, and they came that way because their calls went unanswered. With an appointment Zura waits twenty to thirty minutes and calls it normal.

**Why C sits third.** Real for one person, but it is a predictable monthly chore rather than an urgent gap, and so far only one voice. It stays in the pool.

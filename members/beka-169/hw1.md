### Week 2 · Evidence over opinion

I asked in one interview and logged two. I interviewed my grandmother on 05.10 at her flat with Giorgi logging. She goes every month to renew the same prescription. On her last visit it took several tries to find an appointment that fit, the bus is about an hour each way, and she waited about two hours with an appointment before she had to leave without seeing the doctor. Giorgi watched for places where I answered for her. I was second logger for Gocha at the hospital café and first logger for Zura at his flat, where I kept Giorgi on the calls when Zura jumped ahead to the trip, and made sure the six call-log dates were written down exactly. The thing I learned this week: having an appointment did not tell her when she would actually be seen, and she said that herself at the end, when I asked what I had missed. ([grandmother log](../../01-discovery/interview-logs/01-grandmother-district-clinic.md), [Gocha log](../../01-discovery/interview-logs/02-heart-patient-ward.md), [Zura log](../../01-discovery/interview-logs/03-diabetic-polyclinic.md))

### Week 3 · Kill your favorite idea

My favorite idea was that older patients with a standing treatment often have to make a clinic trip for a repeat prescription or a brief question, and often leave without it. I checked it against the interviews rather than treating every long wait or walk-in as proof. My grandmother's visit directly supported the repeat-prescription part: she waited about two hours with an appointment, left without the prescription, and had to make the trip again (log 01). Gocha and Zura also came in without appointments after calls went unanswered, but their visits were between-visit problems, not repeat-prescription renewals (logs 02 and 03); the doctor confirmed that brief questions can become walk-ins (log 04). I narrowed my claim: only one interview showed a patient leaving without a repeat prescription, so “often leave without it” is not established. This was a useful boundary to preserve, not a reason to overstate the evidence. ([grandmother log](../../01-discovery/interview-logs/01-grandmother-district-clinic.md), [Gocha log](../../01-discovery/interview-logs/02-heart-patient-ward.md), [Zura log](../../01-discovery/interview-logs/03-diabetic-polyclinic.md), [polyclinic doctor log](../../01-discovery/interview-logs/04-polyclinic-doctor.md), [DECISIONS.md](../../DECISIONS.md))

### Week 4 · The prototype is the conversation

My part of the prototype plan is bringing my father into the five-person test (log 05). His experience makes him a useful tester for the booking assumption: he called the clinic for three days, went in person to book, and waited two weeks for an appointment, but waited only 30 minutes on the visit day. The plan asks him to try finding a doctor and booking a future slot without help. That can show whether the demo flow is clear; it cannot show whether real clinic slots are available or whether online booking reduces waiting. ([prototype plan](../../02-design/prototype-plan.md), [father's interview](../../01-discovery/interview-logs/05-father-clinic.md), [DECISIONS.md](../../DECISIONS.md))

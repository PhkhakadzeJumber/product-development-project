## Week 1 · Act like an owner, not a role

My lane in the contract is code, CI and deployment. This week had no code to write, so I did the foundation work instead: my three problem statements in the pool (my uncle and his doctor, a classmate's broken deployment, a friend's lost merge), my outreach target in the contract, and the sign-off. The uncle statement was the one I had actually watched happen more than once, so I pushed for it to stay in the pool when it looked like we would end up with only hospital-waiting problems.

## Week 2 · Evidence over opinion

I drafted the problem pick, the ICP, the hypothesis v1, the script v1 and the log template in one evening so the team had something to argue with instead of a blank page. Then I ran the first real interview myself, by phone, on 06.10, before the script had been audited, because an un-audited interview with real numbers beat a perfect script with none. It gave us 5 or 6 calls over 2 days from one person, and the "they don't give you a direct number" line that every later interview repeated. I also filled the pool with the teammates' statements, set up the outreach tracker, filed the Gocha, Zura and grandmother logs, wrote the Four Filters scorecard and recorded the verdict. Beyond my lane: all of it is discovery, none of it is code. Still owed: I asked and logged the same interview, so I need to be the logger on the next one.
